What is a mouse test?

A mouse test is a diagnostic tool that verifies your mouse's hardware is working correctly. Complete browser-based mouse tests cover: button function (left, right, middle, side buttons all register), scroll wheel (both directions work, no dead spots), double-click detection (single clicks aren't being registered as double), click speed (CPS) (how fast you or your mouse can produce clicks), and input latency (milliseconds from click to registration). The most common mouse problem is switch wear — over time, mouse switches develop a fault where a single physical press registers as two clicks. Symptoms: files opening twice, drag operations dropping, text-editor double-word selection. Test with a slow deliberate 2 CPS click loop; anything above 2 CPS reported means your switch is double-actuating. Other common issues: scroll wheel getting notchy or reversing direction (encoder wear), side buttons not registering (loose ribbon cable), inconsistent DPI (sensor drift). Mouse tests take 2 minutes and diagnose 90% of hardware problems.

Steps

How to do a full mouse test in your browser

  1. 1

    Button test

    Open any page. Left-click, right-click, and middle-click on non-clickable text. All should trigger context menus or highlight actions. Side buttons should navigate browser history.

  2. 2

    Scroll wheel test

    Scroll a long page up and down. Should be smooth in both directions. Test middle-click (autoscroll on Windows).

  3. 3

    Double-click detection

    Open our CPS test. Click slowly and deliberately, targeting 2 CPS. If the test reports 4 or more CPS, your mouse switch is double-firing.

  4. 4

    Click speed test

    Set the CPS test to 10 seconds. Click as fast as you can. This benchmarks both your hand and your mouse's ability to keep up.

  5. 5

    Tracking test

    Move the cursor slowly in a square pattern. Watch for jitter, dead spots, or warping. Use mouseaccuracy.com for a quick precision test.

Common issues

What mouse test results mean

🛡

Double-clicking on single press

Switch wear. Replace the switch (Omron or Kailh D2FC-F-7N, $2 each) or replace the mouse. Optical-switch mice (Razer Viper) don't have this problem.

🔌

Scroll wheel skips or reverses

Encoder wear or debris. Blow compressed air through the wheel. If persistent, encoder replacement or new mouse.

🎮

Side buttons don't work

Loose ribbon cable inside the mouse. Open the case, reseat the connector. Common on 3+ year old mice.

📋

Cursor jumps randomly

Dirty sensor lens (blow air), bad mousepad surface (glossy = bad for optical), or dying sensor (replace mouse).
