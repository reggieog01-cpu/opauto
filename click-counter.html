What is a click counter?

A click counter is a browser tool that increments a running total every time you click. It's the digital equivalent of a physical tally counter (the metal thumb-clicker inventory workers carry). Uses include counting inventory, tallying scores in games or sports, tracking exercise reps, counting cars passing on a road, and measuring how many times you performed a repetitive action. Modern click counters also display clicks per second (CPS) as a rolling rate, doubling as a click-speed benchmark. Advanced click counters offer increment step size (count by 1, 5, 10, etc.), decrement (subtract on right-click), reset, session save (browser localStorage persists your count across visits), and shareable permalinks (URL-encoded state for sending your count to others). Click counters are used in retail (inventory), scientific research (behavioral observation), sports (game scoring), and gaming (idle games, click-speed practice).

Features

What makes a good click counter?

🔢

Large clickable area

Big button or full-screen click zone. Should work with both mouse and touchscreen tap.

🔁

Reset button

One-tap zero-out. Prevents accidental double-reset with a confirm prompt.

⏮

Undo / decrement

Miscounted? Right-click or tap decrement to subtract 1. Essential for accurate counts.

💾

Session save

Count persists across page refresh via localStorage. Don't lose your count from an accidental tab close.

🕑

Optional CPS display

Rolling rate over the last 5 or 10 seconds. Doubles as a click-speed benchmark.

🔗

Share via URL

URL-encoded state lets you send your count via link.
