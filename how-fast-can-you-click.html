How fast can a human click?

The average adult clicks at 4–7 CPS in a 5–10 second test. This is the benchmark for untrained regular users. With technique, speeds increase significantly: jitter clicking (forearm tension vibration) reaches 10–14 CPS; butterfly clicking (two-finger alternation) reaches 15–22 CPS; drag clicking (mouse-button friction) registers 25–35+ CPS in short bursts. These techniques have practical ceilings — drag clicking wears switches fast and is banned on most competitive Minecraft servers. The absolute human ceiling in real-world testing is around 17 CPS sustained (jitter) or 22 CPS burst (butterfly). Auto clickers like OP Auto Clicker bypass muscle speed entirely and reach 100–120 CPS — the Windows SendInput API ceiling, not a software limit. Research on voluntary finger movement suggests the physiological maximum for a single finger is approximately 7–9 Hz under ideal conditions, which explains why standard clicking (4–7 CPS) plateaus well below the 14–22 CPS achievable through jitter and butterfly techniques that use vibration or two-finger alternation rather than single-finger actuation.
